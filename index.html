PROJECT 1

From Riemann sums to 3D prints

A Riemann sum approximates the area under a curve by adding the areas of many rectangles.

\( \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x \)

as \(n \to \infty\)    ↓

\( \int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} f(x_i^*)\,\Delta x \)

More rectangles → better approximation → the integral.

PROJECT 1

Students turned Riemann sums into objects

They did not have to build the program from scratch.

SEQUENCES AND SERIES

Sequence

An ordered list of terms

\(1,\ \frac12,\ \frac14,\ \frac18,\ldots\)

Series

The sum of those terms

\(1+\frac12+\frac14+\frac18+\cdots = 2\)

A sequence gives the terms. A series asks what happens when we add them.

THE ONE EXAMPLE I STILL GIVE

Sierpiński Carpet

Divide a square into nine parts, remove the center, and repeat the process on every remaining square.

Area after \(n\) steps

\(A_n=\left(\frac{8}{9}\right)^n\)

Each step keeps

\(\frac{8}{9}\)

The Sierpiński carpet, printed.
Side length \(=3\), so the original area is \(9\).

AREA AFTER \(n\) STAGES

\(A_n=9\left(\frac89\right)^n\)

Each stage keeps \(\frac89\) of the previous area.
